Threat Landscape

Common Cyber Threats

Attackers exploit human trust, urgency, and software weaknesses. Learn how each threat operates, recognize the red flags, and understand realistic scenarios.

1. Phishing

Deceptive emails, SMS (smishing), or direct messages disguised as legitimate institutions designed to trick you into revealing passwords, credit card info, or OTPs.

What it is

Cybercriminals impersonate well-known companies, postal services, or banks to steal your confidential information or login credentials.

Warning Signs

Urgent threats ("Account suspended in 24 hours"), generic greetings ("Dear Customer"), mismatched URLs, and spelling errors in the sender address.

How to Protect Yourself

Never click links in unsolicited messages. Inspect the sender domain carefully and verify notices directly via official apps or verified phone numbers.

Realistic Scenario: Sri Lanka

Receiving an SMS purporting to be from a postal or courier service claiming an international parcel is held due to an incomplete address. Clicking the link opens a lookalike portal asking for a Rs. 350 customs fee, prompting for credit card details and an SMS OTP.

2. Malware

Malicious software—including spyware, trojans, keyloggers, and viruses—engineered to infiltrate your device, harvest keystrokes, and extract private data silently.

What it is

Hostile software designed to gain unauthorized access to a computer or mobile phone, often disguised as legitimate programs or pirated utilities.

Warning Signs

Unexpected battery drain, unexplained mobile data spikes, persistent pop-up advertisements, slow performance, or unknown apps appearing on your home screen.

How to Protect Yourself

Install software only from official app stores (Google Play, Apple App Store). Keep operating systems updated, and never download cracked tools or third-party APKs.

Realistic Scenario: Sri Lanka

Downloading "Modded WhatsApp APKs" or cracked exam software shared via Telegram channels. These modified packages secretly bundle spyware that monitors SMS verification codes and extracts stored photo galleries.

3. Online Scams

Deceptive schemes exploiting financial hardship, curiosity, or greed—including fake work-from-home tasks, lottery prizes, and bogus investment schemes.

What it is

Fraudulent schemes orchestrated to trick victims into sending money under the guise of guaranteed investment profits, part-time jobs, or lottery winnings.

Warning Signs

Promises of abnormally high daily earnings with zero effort, demands for upfront deposits or recharge fees to unlock payments, and pressure to act immediately.

How to Protect Yourself

Remember: legitimate employers never ask job seekers to deposit money. Verify corporate entities independently and never transfer funds to personal bank accounts.

Realistic Scenario: Sri Lanka

Receiving an unprompted WhatsApp or Telegram message offering Rs. 5,000–12,000 daily for liking YouTube videos or reviewing Google maps locations. Once you complete trial tasks, scammers demand an upfront deposit to withdraw your supposed earnings.

4. Social Engineering

Psychological manipulation that exploits human emotion, fear, or authority to convince victims to willingly bypass security protocols and surrender sensitive codes.

What it is

Attacking the human element rather than hardware, using pretexting, impersonation, or manufactured crisis scenarios to manipulate targets.

Warning Signs

Callers insisting on secrecy, fabricating urgent emergencies regarding family or bank accounts, and demanding immediate disclosure of verification numbers.

How to Protect Yourself

Always pause and independently verify. Hang up and contact your bank or family member directly using a trusted, saved telephone number.

Realistic Scenario: Sri Lanka

A fraudster calls pretending to be a bank security officer or telecom executive claiming an unauthorized fund transfer is occurring. They ask you to read out the 6-digit SMS OTP you just received to "cancel" the transaction, while in reality using it to empty your account.

5. Ransomware

Malicious extortion software that scrambles and encrypts your valuable documents, databases, and photos, demanding cryptocurrency payments for the decryption key.

What it is

A high-impact malware strain that locks files using strong mathematical encryption, rendering critical organizational or personal data inaccessible.

Warning Signs

Files suddenly failing to open with altered extensions (e.g., .locked, .crypto), desktop wallpapers replaced with ransom instructions, and high disk usage.

How to Protect Yourself

Maintain the 3-2-1 backup strategy (three copies, two different media types, one offline). Keep software patched and disable Microsoft Office document macros.

Realistic Scenario: Sri Lanka

An accounting clerk at a local enterprise opens an email attachment named "Overdue_Invoice_September.zip" containing a disguised executable. Within minutes, all shared company spreadsheets and accounting databases on the local network become encrypted.

6. Public Wi-Fi Risks

Unencrypted or rogue wireless networks in coffee shops, airports, and hotels that expose your browsing traffic to packet sniffing and man-in-the-middle interception.

What it is

Open wireless connections that lack encryption, allowing nearby cyber attackers on the same network to intercept unencrypted traffic or deploy evil-twin hotspots.

Warning Signs

Wi-Fi hotspots requiring no password, duplicate network names (e.g., "Cafe_Free_WiFi" vs "Cafe_Guest"), or browser warnings regarding invalid SSL certificates.

How to Protect Yourself

Avoid logging into bank accounts or sensitive services over public Wi-Fi. Use personal mobile hotspot data or connect through a reputable Virtual Private Network (VPN).

Realistic Scenario: Sri Lanka

Connecting to an unsecured hotspot named "Station_Free_WiFi" while waiting for an intercity train. A malicious actor on the network operates a rogue DNS server that redirects login requests to counterfeit banking pages.

Actionable Defenses

Practical Cyber Safety Tips

Explore everyday digital hygiene practices tailored for Sri Lankan students, remote workers, and families. Filter by topic to find specific guidelines.

Passwords

Adopt Multi-Word Passphrases

Instead of short, predictable words with replaced letters, combine four or more unrelated words (e.g. lotus-monsoon-tea-ocean). Length is the most critical defense against automated brute-force attacks.

Passwords

Use a Password Manager

Humans cannot safely memorize dozens of unique 16-character passwords. Reputable password managers generate and encrypt random passwords, autofilling them only on verified authentic websites.

Passwords

Stop Reusing Passwords

When one company suffers a database breach, attackers use automated bots to test those identical email-and-password combinations across email providers, social media, and banking services.

Accounts

Enable Multi-Factor Authentication

MFA requires two proofs of identity before granting access. Even if an attacker steals your password, they cannot access your account without your authenticator app or hardware key.

Accounts

Treat OTPs as Strict Secrets

One-Time Passwords (OTPs) authorize high-stakes transactions. Official bank staff, customer service agents, and telecoms will never ask you to read out your OTP over the phone.

Accounts

Audit Connected Apps Regularly

Periodically review third-party apps linked to your Google, Facebook, or Apple IDs. Revoke permissions for older games, quizzes, and utilities you no longer actively use.

Browsing

Inspect URLs & HTTPS Limits

The padlock icon in your browser only proves the connection is encrypted in transit—it does not mean the website is legitimate. Scammers obtain free SSL certificates for phishing domains too.

Browsing

Keep Browsers Updated Promptly

Zero-day vulnerabilities in web engines are patched frequently by browser vendors. Enable automatic updates on Chrome, Firefox, Safari, and Edge to defend against drive-by downloads.

Browsing

Avoid Risky Downloads on Public Wi-Fi

Never install software updates or browser extensions while connected to open public Wi-Fi networks. Attackers can hijack network requests and substitute malicious payload executables.

Privacy

Limit Personal Info on Social Media

Avoid posting photos of flight boarding passes, national identity cards, utility bills, or full birthdays. Attackers aggregate this data to bypass security challenge questions and impersonate you.

Privacy

Restrict Mobile App Permissions

Check app permissions on your Android or iOS device. Deny location, microphone, contacts, and camera access to apps that have no operational need for those capabilities.

Privacy

Spot Emotional & Urgency Traps

Scammers manufacture fear ("Arrest warrant issued"), urgency ("Pay in 1 hour"), or excitement ("You won Rs. 500,000"). When you feel an intense emotional push to act, stop and verify.

Discovered Red Flags Breakdown:

Undiscovered Flag #1
Suspicious Sender Address

The domain sl-postal-secure-track-delivery.xyz uses an obscure generic TLD (.xyz) with hyphenated words. Legitimate government and postal entities in Sri Lanka operate under verified .gov.lk or accredited commercial domains.

Undiscovered Flag #2
Generic Impersonal Greeting

Phishing emails use generic placeholders like "Dear Valued Customer" because they are mass-blasted to millions of harvested email addresses. Couriers that have your parcel already know your name.

Undiscovered Flag #3
Manufactured Urgency

Threatening that your package will be "destroyed within 24 hours" is an emotional pressure tactic. Attackers manufacture artificial urgency to bypass your rational thinking and prevent verification.

Undiscovered Flag #4
Insecure Link & Fake Domain

The link uses unencrypted http:// and directs to a foreign .cc domain. Legitimate delivery tracking portals encrypt sessions with modern HTTPS and host under official company domains.

Undiscovered Flag #5
Unusual Request for CVV, NIC & OTP

A postal courier will never ask for your card CVV code, National Identity Card, or SMS One-Time Passwords via email. Surrendering your OTP gives criminals authorization to execute unauthorized transactions.

Incident Support

Report an Incident & Get Help

If you suspect your accounts, smartphone, or money have been compromised, act swiftly. Follow these immediate emergency steps and report the incident to verified national authorities.

Verified Official Authority

Sri Lanka CERT|CC

The Computer Emergency Readiness Team | Coordination Center (Sri Lanka CERT) is the national focal point for cybersecurity incidents, offering coordinated response, threat mitigation, and technical advisories across Sri Lanka.

https://cert.gov.lk/report_incident

Report phishing sites, hacked social accounts, malware infections, ransomware extortion, and online harassment directly through their official reporting portal.

Open Sri Lanka CERT Incident Portal
Opens official cert.gov.lk portal in a new tab

Sri Lanka Police Cyber Crime Division (CID)

If you have suffered financial loss, bank account theft, or digital blackmail, file a formal complaint with the Criminal Investigation Department (CID) Cyber Crime Division. Bring all transaction receipts and printed message transcripts.

Bank Financial Fraud Hotline

If you accidentally submitted debit/credit card details or an SMS OTP on a phishing website, call your bank's 24/7 emergency customer care hotline immediately to freeze cards and stop pending transfers.

Platform Abuse Reporting

Report suspicious WhatsApp or Telegram accounts using the built-in "Report Spam" button. Flag fake websites via Google Safe Browsing to protect other internet users globally.

Immediate Emergency Response Plan

1

Disconnect Device

Immediately disable Wi-Fi and mobile data to prevent malware from transmitting your files or receiving command instructions.

2

Freeze Bank Cards

If card or bank details were entered, call your bank immediately to block affected cards and reverse pending transactions.

3

Change Passwords

From a separate, uncompromised device, update master passwords for primary email, social media, and banking accounts.

4

Preserve Evidence

Take high-resolution screenshots of fraudulent text messages, payment slips, sender phone numbers, and URLs for official reporting.
